# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

static HTML/CSS/vanilla JS — user's choice for a landing page; no build step, opens directly in a browser

## Users

Pemula Indonesia. Adults and students in Indonesia learning Japanese from zero — most with no prior kana, many motivated by anime, games, music, travel, or the prospect of work and study in Japan. They are reading a landing page in Bahasa Indonesia to decide whether to start, usually on a phone.

## Product Purpose

Tamago is a Japanese-learning site whose landing page must do one job: convince a total beginner that starting is safe, structured, and worth the first week. Success means the visitor understands what is taught, sees the first concrete step, and takes it.

Tamago (卵) means "egg" — the beginner's starting state, and the thing that hatches. The brand is an egg that becomes fluent.

## Positioning

Beginners-first scaffolding, not a generic language app. The landing page commits to the honest, specific facts of the journey — the JLPT level ladder with real vocabulary and kanji counts, the three-script system, the hours involved — instead of vague "learn fast" claims. Nobody else's page opens with the true numbers.

## Operating Context

Landing page only. One static page, Indonesian copy, Japanese terms shown in their own script. Sections cover kana, kanji, vocabulary, grammar, listening, and the JLPT ladder. No app, no login, no backend.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Single-page static HTML/CSS, no framework, no build step.
- Material in scope: hiragana, katakana, kanji, kosakata, tata bahasa, listening, JLPT N5–N1.
- Model akses: free core with paid extras — CTA is "Mulai Belajar" (start learning), not "Buy".
- Logo asset: `assets/mascot.png`, sourced from `~/storage/shared/tango-mascot-v2.png` — chibi girl, purple hair, yellow circle hair accessory, green top, holding a Japanese flag, on pale blue ground. Must be used as the brand mark.
- Indonesian copy with authentic Japanese script throughout.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Tamago. Egg motif is binding.
- Mascot asset is binding and is the brand mark.
- Visual world: the user supplied the "Slush" style reference as a binding brief — inflatable sticker universe on pastel paper. Its tokens, components, do's and don'ts are committed and override general taste.

## Evidence on Hand

Verified facts gathered for page content, all from primary or reliable secondary sources:

- JLPT has five levels, N5 (easiest) to N1 (hardest). Official source: jlpt.jp.
- Cumulative vocabulary per level (unofficial community estimates, since the JLPT stopped publishing official lists in 2010): N5 ~800, N4 ~1,500, N3 ~3,750, N2 ~6,000, N1 ~10,000.
- Cumulative kanji per level: N5 ~100, N4 ~300, N3 ~650, N2 ~1,000, N1 ~2,000+. N1 covers essentially the 2,136 jōyō kanji.
- Rough school equivalence: N5 ≈ 1st–2nd grade elementary, N4 ≈ 3rd–4th grade, N3 ≈ ~6th grade, N2 ≈ middle school, N1 ≈ high school graduate.
- JLPT is 100% multiple choice at every level — kanji are never handwritten on the exam.
- Test sections and times: N5 vocabulary 20min / grammar·reading 40min / listening 30min; N4 25/55/35; N3 30/70/40; N2 105/50; N1 110/55.
- 2026 test dates: Sunday July 5 and Sunday December 6.
- The most frequent 500 kanji cover ~80% of characters in running text; the top 1,000 push past 90%.
- Hiragana: 48 base characters after the 1900 script reform, arranged in the gojūon ("fifty sounds") grid; developed from cursive kanji via man'yōgana. Katakana is the parallel kana syllabary.
- The JLPT does not test speaking at all.

Absences future work must not fabricate: no learner counts, no testimonials, no ratings, no pricing, no partner institutions, no named teachers.

## Product Principles

- Honest numbers over hype. The real level ladder and real counts are the persuasion.
- The first step is small and visible. A beginner leaves knowing exactly what to do first.
- Japanese script is shown, not described. Kana and kanji appear on the page.
- Indonesian-language warmth, Japanese-language accuracy.
- The egg is the mascot and the metaphor; it appears as the mark, never as decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Body and UI text needs 4.5:1 contrast minimum. The Slush palette's black-on-pastel satisfies this; large display text satisfies 3:1. Sticker accents stay decorative and never carry information alone. Real focus rings, keyboard-navigable nav and buttons, and reduced-motion handling for the marquee.
